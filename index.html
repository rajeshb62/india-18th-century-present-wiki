India 18th Century–Present

From the late Mughal order through Company sovereignty, Crown rule, and the political settlements of the twentieth century, India sat at the meeting of its own long civilizational continuum and the institutions, techniques, and ideas that arrived with European sea power. This knowledge base gathers sources that record that meeting as their authors saw it: law and education, press and railways, caste and Islam, missions and vernacular literature, and the smaller reverse current of Indian influence on the West.

Two Chatham House texts from the early 1940s sit on the shelf: O’Malley’s 1941 symposium and Whyte’s 1943 wartime Bird’s-Eye View. Beside them are B. R. Ambedkar’s Pakistan or the Partition of India (2nd ed. 1945; AMS 1975 reprint) and Maulana Abul Kalam Azad’s India Wins Freedom (complete version): the analytic case for Pakistan and a Congress president’s narrative of how freedom came with partition. Sir John Malcolm’s three-volume Life of Robert, Lord Clive (1836): Arcot, Plassey, the Diwani, and Clive’s last years. The earliest pamphlet on this shelf is Francis Russell’s 1793 Short History of the East India Company, written as the exclusive-trade term ran out. John Bruce’s official Annals (1810) are now complete in three volumes (1600–1707/8). James Mill’s History of British India, Wilson’s Madden set, is now complete in nine volumes: Mill through 1805 (vols I–VI) and Wilson 1805–1835 (vols VII–IX: 1813 charter; Hastings; Amherst/Burma/Bentinck and the 1833 charter). Beside them is Lucien Wolf’s 1921 Life of the First Marquess of Ripon, now complete in two volumes (to 1880; then the viceroyalty and last years to 1909). Sir Richard Temple’s India in 1880 (Murray, 3rd ed. 1881) surveys the empire at that same hinge — land, railways, famine, Russia, finance — from a Governor of Bombay and Finance Minister’s experience.

Sources

Scholarly surveys / Chatham House studies

  • Modern India and the West: A Study of the Interaction of Their Civilizations (O’Malley, ed., OUP for RIIA, 1941; lithographic reprint 1968) — Sixteen-chapter cooperative survey of Western impact on India since the sixteenth century (law, education, press, transport, economy, Christian ethic, Hinduism, caste, Islam, tribes, women, six vernacular literatures) and of Indian influence on the West, with O’Malley’s historical background, impact narrative, and closing general survey.
  • India: A Bird’s-Eye View (Whyte, RIIA / OUP 1943) — Short wartime briefing: historic India, the British Raj from the 1600 Company charter through the 1935 Act, Cripps and the 1942 crisis (Pakistan, Quit India), the Indian States and paramountcy, and India’s war effort, with parallel dates and statistical tables.

Partition / wartime political treatises

  • Pakistan or the Partition of India (Ambedkar, 2nd ed. 1945; AMS Press 1975) — Muslim case, Hindu objections, Hardayal and guessed Muslim parity alternatives, Turkey/Czechoslovakia lessons, communal malaise, and Part V on whether Pakistan must be, practical problems, and who can decide. Main text complete; appendices I–VII only in this EPUB.
  • India Wins Freedom (Azad / Humayun Kabir; Orient Blackswan complete version, sealed passages restored 1988) — Autobiographical narrative 1937–48: Congress ministries, wartime presidency, Cripps, Quit India, Ahmednagar, Simla, Cabinet Mission, Interim Government, Mountbatten, partition; Azad’s case that the Cabinet Mission Plan could have held India together.

Company records / Bruce

  • Annals of the Honorable East-India Company, Vol. I (Bruce, 1810) — 1600 through Charles II’s Restoration charter (1661): Portuguese and Dutch frame, first voyages, Surat, Roe, joint stocks, Courten and Interregnum.
  • Annals of the Honorable East-India Company, Vol. II (Bruce, 1810) — Chapter II, 1660–1 to 1688–9: Aurangzeb, Sivaji, Restoration charters, year-by-year to the Revolution.
  • Annals of the Honorable East-India Company, Vol. III (Bruce, 1810) — Chapter III, 1688–89 to the union of the London and English companies 1707–8. Completes the 1810 set.

Histories / Mill–Wilson

  • The History of British India, Vol. I (Mill; 4th ed. with notes by H. H. Wilson, Madden 1840) — Book I, British intercourse to Godolphin 1711; Book II, Of the Hindus (chronology, caste, government, laws, taxes, religion, manners). Vol. I of eight.
  • The History of British India, Vol. II (Mill/Wilson, 1840) — Book II continued (arts, literature, general reflections on Hindu civilization); Book III, The Mohammedans (Ghaznavids, Ghurids/Afghans, Mughal dynasty, comparison of Muslim and Hindu civilization). Vol. II of eight.
  • The History of British India, Vol. III (Mill/Wilson, 1840) — Book IV, exclusive Company 1708 to the Regulating Act 1773 (India House, Carnatic, Plassey, Lally, Meer Jafar/Mir Qasim, Diwani, Circars and Hyder, parliamentary reconstruction); Book V chs I–II, Hastings to Nuncomar. Vol. III of eight.
  • The History of British India, Vol. IV (Mill/Wilson, 1840) — Book V chs III–IX: Hastings after Nuncomar (revenue, Carnatic/Tanjore/Pigot, Hyder and Tippoo, Goddard and the Supreme Court, Benares, Oudh and the Begums, Dundas/Fox/Pitt). Completes Book V. Vol. IV of eight.
  • The History of British India, Vol. V (Mill/Wilson, 1840) — Book VI chs I–VI: Macpherson, Board of Control and Arcot debts, Cornwallis, Hastings impeachment and trial, third Mysore war, Permanent Settlement and its results. Vol. V of eight.
  • The History of British India, Vol. VI (Mill/Wilson, 1840) — Book VI chs VII–XIII: 1793 charter, Shore, Wellesley (Seringapatam, Oudh, Surat/Tanjore/Arcot), Bassein, Lake and Assye, Holkar, Cornwallis/Barlow. Completes Book VI and Mill’s narrative to 1805. Vol. VI of eight.
  • The History of British India, Vol. VII (Wilson continuation, Madden 1845) — Wilson Book I chs I–VIII: 1805–1813 (Barlow, Vellore, Minto, Travancore and the Madras officers, Java and Mauritius, dakoiti and revenue, 1813 charter). Vol. VII of the set.
  • The History of British India, Vol. VIII (Wilson continuation, Madden and Malcolm 1846) — Wilson Book II chs I–XII: 1813–1823, Marquis of Hastings (Nepal war, Kandy, Pindaris, last Maratha war, Central India, courts/revenue/press). Vol. VIII of nine.
  • The History of British India, Vol. IX (Wilson continuation, Madden 1848) — Wilson Book III / Volume III: 1823–1835, Adam and Amherst, first Burma war, Bhurtpore, Bentinck (suttee, thugs, English education), Coorg and Mysore, 1833 charter. Completes the nine-volume set.

Company pamphlets / 1793 charter

  • A Short History of the East India Company (Russell, 2nd ed., Sewell and Debrett 1793) — Origin and union of the old and new companies, India-House 3 per cent. annuities, purchased vs conquered forts, expenses of conquest, exclusive trade and the “monopoly” charge, foreign rivals, India and China commerce, the Company as sovereign and merchant, a regulated opening for private trade, Masulipatam and the Northern Circars, four-year sales, abridgment of the 1793 Act.

Contemporary lives / Viceroys

  • Life of the First Marquess of Ripon, Vol. I (Wolf, John Murray 1921) — Birth in Downing Street 1827 through Commons, War Office, Cabinet, Alabama claims, conversion to Rome, Gladstone’s Catholic Viceroy. Indian apprenticeship 1857–58. Vol. I of two.
  • Life of the First Marquess of Ripon, Vol. II (Wolf, John Murray 1921) — Viceroyalty 1880–84 (Afghanistan, Kandahar, Baring, local self-government, Vernacular Press Act, Ilbert Bill) and elder statesman to 1909. Completes the Life. Vol. II of two.

Contemporary surveys / Official India

  • India in 1880 (Temple, John Murray, 3rd ed. 1881) — Empire survey at Lytton/Ripon: Native States, land and revenues, canals and railways, famine, missions, army, Russia/Afghanistan, finance. Complete single volume.
  • Indian Musalmáns (W. Nassau Lees, Williams and Norgate 1871) — Three Times letters after Justice Norman’s assassination (loyalty, discontent, redress); Mayo’s 1871 Muslim-education resolution; critique of Macaulay’s 1835 Minute (printed in appendix). ZIP metadata says Hunter; text is Lees.

Contemporary lives / Company India

  • The Life of Robert, Lord Clive, Vol. I (Malcolm, John Murray 1836) — Clive’s family and boyhood, Carnatic wars and Arcot, return to England, Gheriah, Calcutta retaken, Chandernagore, Plassey, Mir Jafar, Northern Circars and the jaghir. Copy from the Great Britain 18th century wiki.
  • The Life of Robert, Lord Clive, Vol. II (Malcolm, 1836) — 1758–1767: Lally and Madras, the Dutch in Bengal, India House politics, second Bengal government, the Diwani of Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa.
  • The Life of Robert, Lord Clive, Vol. III (Malcolm, 1836) — Last years 1766–1774: white mutiny of the Bengal army, salt monopoly, parliamentary inquiry, Clive’s death.
